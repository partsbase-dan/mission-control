How each workshop runs

45 minutes: 5 to frame the session, 25 of discussion, 10 to rank the asks and 5 to agree on next steps.

Sales will share real examples (recent wins and losses, common objections, the buyers they talk to most), the assets they use today, and their top request for Marketing with what it would change in a deal.

Ground rules: speak for the customer, with specific deals over general views. Stay on that session's product group; anything else goes on the findings list for later.

Questions for every workshop

  1. Who buys this, and who decides? Two or three recent examples.
  2. Why do customers buy, and why do they say no?
  3. Where do deals slow down or stall?
  4. Which leads from Marketing have been good, and which weren't?
  5. What should Marketing do first, and what would it change in a deal?

Workshop guides

These are drafts. Sales can tell us if a question misses what matters for their product.

PB Locator

Who uses it, how Sales positions it, and what would help it sell.

  • Which customer types get the most value from it, and how do they find it today?
  • What do prospects compare it to?
  • Which proof points, like results or customer stories, would help close?

Outcome: a ranked list of audience segments and the top three asks for Marketing.

PartStore

The buyer and seller journey, and where Marketing can raise sign-ups and activation.

  • Who's the ideal PartStore customer today, and how does that differ from who actually signs up?
  • What happens between interest and a live store, and where do people drop off?
  • Which segments should get priority in outbound and email?

Outcome: an agreed description of the ideal PartStore customer and the top three asks for Marketing.

Integration (API)

Who buys API integrations, how the sale differs from other products, and what technical content is missing.

  • Which buyers care about the API, and are they technical, commercial or both?
  • What do prospects ask that Sales can't answer without engineering help?
  • What documentation, demos or case studies would shorten the cycle?

Outcome: a list of content and enablement gaps, and a view on whether the API needs its own campaign.

PB Labs

What PB Labs is, who it's for, and how Marketing should introduce it.

  • How does Sales describe it to a customer in one sentence today?
  • Which customers are early adopters, and what made them try it?
  • What stage is it at, and what feedback or results can we share publicly?

Outcome: a one-sentence positioning draft, an early-adopter profile and the top three asks for Marketing.

PB Government Data

The government-data audience, how buyers are reached, and any limits on how Marketing can promote it.

  • Who are the target buyers, and how are they usually found?
  • Are there compliance, procurement or messaging limits we have to respect?
  • Which lists, events or channels does Sales already use for this audience?

Outcome: a target-buyer profile, a list of messaging constraints and the top three asks for Marketing.
