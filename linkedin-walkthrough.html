The loop

What happens, end to end

Six stages. The engine does the production work; the one place a person is required is the approval gate — and that's deliberate.

01 Campaign brief Audience, offer, proof points. Here: Distributors → “Exhibit at PBExpo 2027.”
02 AI copy — 4 variants Claude drafts on-brand headline + body + CTA options against the brief.
03 Rendered ads Copy flows into a fixed Figma template → four 1200×627 renders.
04 Automated QA Length, brand, link checks. This set: 4/4 passed, 0 blocking.
05 Teams approval Card lands in Teams. Per-variant verdict + feedback, then Submit. ◆ Human hard stop
06 Live on LinkedIn Approved set launches with tags intact. Phase 7 — pending API credentials.
→ Runs entirely on internal tools — Claude, Figma, Teams, OneDrive, Power Automate. The loop ends at “approved”; today's demo stops there.

The case

What it replaces

Before
  • Creative produced by hand, one ad at a time.
  • Approvals happen ad-hoc over email and chat — no record of who approved what.
  • Tagging is inconsistent, so we can't reliably say which copy worked.
After
  • Brief in → on-brand options out, a full set at once.
  • One Teams tap to approve, and every decision is logged.
  • Every variant tagged at creation — attribution is built in, not bolted on.
The control story

Guardrails

Automation earns trust by where it refuses to act on its own. Four constraints sit under the loop.

Human approval is a hard stop

Nothing goes live without an explicit tap. The engine cannot self-approve or skip the gate.

Every decision is logged

Who approved, what they changed, and when — a complete, reviewable trail for each package.

The engine is the only writer

One controlled process makes every change and refuses to act on anything that isn't approved.

Kill-switch + spend circuit-breaker

A stop control and a spend ceiling sit beneath the launch step for when it reaches Phase 7.

Why it compounds

Measurable by design

Templates stay fixed; copy varies. Every variant is tagged the moment it's created, so performance maps straight back to a specific creative.

utm_content = <template>_<variant>
test-pbexpo_v0 test-pbexpo_v1 test-pbexpo_v2 test-pbexpo_v3

→ We can say which copy wins, per template, per campaign — and feed that back into the next round of drafts. The A/B set isn't guessed; it's decided at approval time, because the approver keeps the strong variants, requests changes on others, and kills the rest.

Live demo

What you'll see on screen

Four moves. The command is shown for reference — in the meeting it's a single helper script.

1
Fire the approval card
The package posts to Teams — it appears in the chat with the Workflows bot as an approval card.
$ ./demo.sh fire
2
Review & decide in Teams
The card shows all four variants — headline, CTA, the attribution tag, and a link to the renders in Figma. Set a verdict per variant, add feedback, and Submit. For the demo: approve all four; or request changes on one to show the regeneration round-trip.
3
Drain the decision
The loop reads the verdicts — approved variants get promoted; anything with requested changes goes back for a round-2 regen.
$ ./demo.sh watch
4
Show the result
The package moves to “approved,” each variant marked approved — ready to hand to launch once Phase 7 is live.
$ ./demo.sh result
