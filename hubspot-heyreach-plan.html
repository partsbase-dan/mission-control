Import waves: 119,306 in July, 6,916 in August, 6,094 in September. Remaining to import is roughly 48,000 marketing contacts, and that is the whole of what is left. The July 9 audit's separate CRM-only bucket is retired. Where this plan and the July 9 audit disagree, this plan is current.

Suppression: verified clean

Suppression was handled by exclusion at the source. Unsubscribed contacts were never imported. Verified by sampling 25 of the 23,545 records in the Mailchimp unsubscribe export against the portal: zero present. The 25 opt-out records in HubSpot are not a gap, because there is nothing to opt out.

One residual hole worth closing

Those 23,545 exist nowhere in HubSpot, so the portal has no memory that they ever unsubscribed. Any future path that creates a contact, a PBExpo registration form, a Clay push, a list upload, a rep typing someone in, can re-introduce one of them with a clean marketable status and mail them. Exclusion is a one-time filter. It is not a guardrail.

Fix, about 30 minutes

Import all 23,545 as non-marketing contacts with opt-out set to true. They do not count against the marketing tier, and HubSpot then blocks them permanently no matter how they re-enter.

The real constraint: marketing-contact headroom

Assuming the contracted ceiling is 185,000, the figure the July audit worked against:

  • A target of 180,000 leaves 5,000 of headroom.
  • Contacts are entering at roughly 6,000 to 7,000 per month (August 6,916; September 6,094 through the 21st).
  • That is under one month of runway before the tier auto-upgrades and the bill steps up.

Every contact in the portal is currently a marketing contact, 132,160 of 132,319. Of those, 45,115 are billable and not mailable: 28,305 Low Priority plus 16,810 with no ICP tier assigned. Flipping those to non-marketing before finishing the import lands you at roughly 135,000 marketing after the full load, with about 50,000 of genuine headroom instead of 5,000.

Three data issues that shape the segment build

  1. ICP properties came through populated and usable. icp_tier and icp_segment are ready as segment inputs today. This is what keeps the Week 2 builds on schedule.
  2. icp_segment is unassigned on 68,165 contacts, 51.5%. Half the list cannot enter a Distributor, MRO, OEM or Airline segment until Clay runs a company to firmographics pass. That pass takes weeks, so it starts Monday regardless of everything else.
  3. Country is raw and OFAC was not applied. 112,453 "united states" plus 2,733 "usa", plus state names and city strings, plus 10,128 blank. Russia, Belarus and Myanmar records are present as marketing contacts. No geo or GDPR logic works until this is normalized.

Lifecycle stage is Lead on 132,318 of 132,319 records, so there is no lifecycle model yet. That is a Track B dependency, not a blocker.

Track B · Segment library and handoff to Alejandra (week of Sep 29)

The operating principle

You own the segment library. Alejandra builds emails against saved lists and never writes filters. This is the difference between HubSpot and what Mailchimp became: list logic has one owner and an audit trail, and a builder cannot accidentally mail 135,000 people.

Enforce it with permissions, not policy. Alejandra is a contractor moving from ad-hoc one-off Mailchimp sends to a platform she has not used. Give her Marketing access without list-creation or list-edit rights. The rule then holds because HubSpot enforces it, not because she remembered it. It also protects her, because she cannot cause the incident.

Standing risk

She is a single point of failure on all email execution. Worth naming in the monthly update, and worth a second trained person by Q1.

Naming convention

[Brand] – [Layer] – [Name] – [State], for example PB – Audience – MRO – Send Ready and GLOBAL – Suppress – Unsubscribed.

Layer 3 · Engagement state

Engaged, Cold, Never engaged.

Say this to Alejandra explicitly

HubSpot has no native engagement history yet. For the first 60 days these lists run off migrated Mailchimp engagement flags, and 66.4% of the clean list never opened anything. Switch the definitions to native HubSpot engagement at the 60-day mark. Put it on the calendar so the definition does not silently rot.

Layer 4 · Warm-up cohorts

New sending domain, zero reputation, and a source list whose historical complaint rate ran 27% to 30%. Non-negotiable.

  • Weeks 1 and 2: Tier 3 and Tier 1, engaged, send-ready only. Start at 2,000 to 5,000 per day.
  • Double volume every 2 to 3 sends only if complaint rate stays under 0.1% and bounce rate under 2%. Hold on any breach.
  • Cold segments stay untouched for four weeks minimum.
  • Never-engaged is a separate re-permission decision, not a warm-up cohort.

What Alejandra receives

  1. Segment catalog. One page: list name, plain-English definition, current count, intended use, who approves a send to it.
  2. Send request intake. The spec exists already. Stand it up as a form.
  3. Four standing rules. Request a list, never build a filter. All five global suppressions on every send. Every link carries a UTM from the registry. During warm-up, nothing sends without your sign-off.
  4. A 45-minute working session building one real email against one real list, together. Do not hand this over as a document.

Track C · HeyReach

The agency model is the design goal, stated plainly: campaigns are operated centrally, reps contribute identity and take handoffs, not execution. Every decision below gets tested against "does this keep execution off the reps' plate."

Supersedes MeetAlfred

MeetAlfred is still listed as the Phase 2 LinkedIn tool in CLAUDE.md and dashboard-state.json. Both need updating.

Vendor facts in this track were verified against HeyReach's pricing page and help centre on 2026-09-21. Sources are listed at the end of the section.

C1 · Cost, verified

Growth is $79 per sender per month, priced per connected LinkedIn account rather than per human user. Five senders is $395 per month, or $316 billed annually at $63 per sender.

Growth includes everything the agency model needs. No upgrade required:

  • Unified inbox
  • Unlimited campaigns and all LinkedIn actions
  • Integrations, including Clay and HubSpot
  • API and webhooks
  • Workspaces and permissions
  • MCP server
  • 100 enrichment credits per sender, 500 per month across five. Clay is the primary enrichment source, so treat these as incidental.

The Agency tier at $999 per month for 25 senders only becomes cheaper per sender above about 13 senders. Not relevant at five.

Sales Navigator is not required

Free and Premium LinkedIn accounts both run HeyReach campaigns. Sales Navigator is mandatory only for the InMail action. Premium, around $80 to $99 per seat per month, buys unlimited personalized connection requests with a 300-character note plus advanced search filters. Worth having, not a prerequisite. Since lists come from Clay rather than Sales Nav search, check what the five reps already carry before budgeting anything here.

Correction to the earlier draft: it stated Sales Navigator was required on every seat, at about $500 per month. That was wrong.

C2 · Sender model: five rep profiles

Five reps' real LinkedIn profiles gives the best acceptance and reply rates, real people with real aviation networks. It also makes the consent and acceptable-use policy mandatory, and it needs to exist before procurement closes or it becomes the thing that stalls launch in week three.

The policy needs to cover, in writing, signed by each rep:

  • Explicit consent to send on their behalf, and what gets sent.
  • How account access is handled, and that it ends when they leave the role.
  • Who reads replies. This is the clause reps actually care about, because outreach replies land in their personal LinkedIn inbox next to recruiter messages and personal conversations.
  • Their right to review sequence copy before it goes out under their name.
  • A clean off-ramp. Any rep can withdraw and the campaign reroutes across the remaining pool.

Loop in HR before circulating. This is personal-account access, not just an IT question.

C3 · Capacity and account safety

HeyReach's own guidance and the LinkedIn norm both land at 20 to 25 connection requests per sender per day. Across five senders that is 100 to 125 per day, roughly 500 to 625 per week. Size campaign one to that number, not to the size of the list.

Limits are shared across campaigns, not per campaign

A sender enrolled in three campaigns at a 20 per day limit delivers about 7 per campaign per day. So "one active campaign per sender at launch" is not only a safety rule. Running two campaigns on the same pool halves the throughput of each. Add concurrency only when you have enough senders to absorb the split.

  • Warm each profile before campaign volume. Do not point a freshly connected account at a 500-person list.
  • HeyReach assigns a dedicated non-rotating residential IP per account and auto-freezes an account approaching a risky threshold. Verify both are active per sender at setup.

C4 · List source: Clay to HeyReach, never Sales Nav to HeyReach

Clay is the enrichment backbone and holds the T1 filter logic. Building lists inside HeyReach from Sales Nav searches forks the ICP definition into two places within a month. One source of truth, pushed out.

The requirement that will quietly break attribution if missed

HeyReach only creates or updates a HubSpot contact if the lead has an email address. LinkedIn prospecting frequently has no email. So Clay must resolve an email for every lead before it is pushed to HeyReach, otherwise those leads run the campaign, generate replies, and log nothing to HubSpot. Make email-found a filter on the Clay export, not a nice-to-have.

C5 · HubSpot integration, before campaign 1

Direction is one-way, HeyReach to HubSpot. This is the vendor's recommendation, and community reports are that attempting two-way sync produces duplicate records within days. Do not build it two-way.

Correction to the earlier draft: it said suppression should sync in both directions. It should not.

SettingConfiguration
Sync triggerOn first reply (default and recommended) or on campaign enrolment. Start with first-reply to keep the CRM clean during the pilot.
Activity mappingsTen event types log to the contact timeline: connection request sent, accepted and withdrawn, messages, InMails, replies, campaign enrolment and pausing, list add and remove, third-party transfers. Each carries campaign name, sender, message body and list name.

Suppression in the other direction is not a native sync

HeyReach exclude lists are lists imported into the HeyReach workspace and selected per campaign. Three ways to feed them:

1. ManualPilot

Export the HubSpot suppression list, import as a HeyReach exclude list, refresh on a fixed cadence. Fine for the pilot, a standing risk afterwards.

2. OutboundSync

Third-party paid connector that pushes a HubSpot list straight into HeyReach exclusion lists. Works, adds a vendor.

3. Build itBy Oct

HeyReach's API and MCP server are both on the Growth plan. A scheduled job that reads HubSpot suppression lists and refreshes the HeyReach exclude list keeps it in-house.

Whichever you pick, the exclude list must carry current customers, unsubscribes, open deals, and anyone already in an active email sequence.

C6 · The MCP server is what makes this a one-person agency

This is on the Growth plan and it is worth more to you than to anyone else on the team. HeyReach's MCP server connects an AI agent directly to the workspace, covering campaigns, leads, lists, replies and webhooks, so the daily operating work runs from a chat window instead of the dashboard: pull campaign performance, filter a list by title and industry, review and triage replies, draft responses, run sentiment on a reply set, move prospects between campaigns.

That is precisely the labour that would otherwise force a coordinator hire to make the agency model work at five senders. Wire it up in week two alongside the HubSpot integration. It also gives you the API path for the suppression job in C5.

C7 · The operating model

Intake (request form)
  -> Dan builds the audience in Clay against the ICP filter (email-found required)
  -> Sequence copy drafted and approved
  -> Rep reviews copy going out under their name
  -> Campaign runs across the 5-sender pool, one campaign per sender at launch
  -> Unified inbox triaged daily (MCP-assisted)
  -> Positive reply -> HubSpot contact + task to the owning rep, 4-hour SLA
  -> Rep takes the conversation from there

Reps execute nothing. That is the pitch to leadership, and it is what makes adoption work where rep-driven sequencing failed before.

C8 · First two campaigns

  1. PBExpo 2027 exec outreach. Net-new exec sequences are written and reviewed already. Fastest path to live with real copy.
  2. T1 supplier acquisition. MRO, Distributor, Overhaul Shop, Regional Airline, not currently on PartsBase.

At 500 to 625 connection requests per week across the pool, run these sequentially, not in parallel.

C9 · Metrics

Connection acceptance rate, reply rate, positive reply rate, meetings booked, pipeline influenced.

Set the v1 target as meetings booked per sender per week. It is the one number that survives a leadership conversation, and it scales cleanly as senders are added.
