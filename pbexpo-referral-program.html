The problem

Registration runs on Launchpad by Swapcard. No Swapcard product offers attendee referrals. Their "referral program" is for organizers referring other organizers. We looked at buying a dedicated tool and at building our own, and found a third option: a Launchpad feature we already have does the tracking.

The answer

Launchpad Marketing Codes. A code in the registration link (?_mc=REF-00417) is saved on the registrant's record. We give every registrant their own code, so every referral is traceable back to the person who shared it.

How it works behind the scenes

Launchpad
Load 10,000 codes onceREF-00001 to REF-10000, imported from one CSV. They belong to nobody yet. Unused codes are cleared after the show.
Launchpad
Someone registersIf they came through a referral link, their record carries the referrer's code.
Zapier
Hand out the next codeA counter gives the new registrant the next unused code, e.g. registrant 417 gets REF-00417. Both codes go to HubSpot: their own and the one they registered with.
HubSpot
Credit the referrerA workflow finds the contact who owns that code, links the two records and adds one to that person's referral count, but only if the new registrant has a qualifying job title.
HubSpot
Send the link and keep scoreThe new registrant gets their own link by email. A report sorted by referral count is the leaderboard.
Why pre-load the codes

Launchpad has to recognize a code before anyone uses it. Loading them all up front means nobody creates codes by hand as registrations come in. Each person still gets a code of their own.

The one catch

Tested: the code does not persist

If someone clicks a referral link, leaves, and comes back to register later without the link, the referral is lost. So the promotion has to tell people to register through the link itself.

Wording for every share message and landing page

Register using your colleague's link, not the homepage. If you leave and come back later, click the link again so they get credit.

Two safeguards

  • Links go straight to the registration form, not the PBExpo homepage. Fewer pages means fewer chances to wander off.
  • An optional "Who referred you?" field on the form. If the code is missing but a name is there, marketing credits the referral by hand.

Rewards To decide

Registration is free, so discounts are off the table. Rewards have to be perks or prizes.

IdeaNotes
VIP perks for referrersLounge access, priority matchmaking slots, recognition on the leaderboard. Low cost, and they matter at the show itself.
Prizes for the top referrersA prize promotion needs legal review. Put it through legal together with the AirPods registration draw (Nov 9–30) so both are signed off at once.
Exhibitors as referrersExhibitors invite their own customers with the same kind of link. It brings in buyers, which is exactly what exhibitors pay for, and becomes a selling point for booth sales.
Only qualified referrals count

Last year about 14% of MRO-segment registrants were students, and airline registrants skewed toward pilots and cabin crew. Crediting only registrants with qualifying job titles keeps the program from rewarding low-value sign-ups.
