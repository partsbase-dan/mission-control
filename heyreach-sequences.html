HeyReach LinkedIn Sequences

Two LinkedIn sequences run centrally through HeyReach across five sales rep profiles. PBExpo sells booth space to the supply side. PBLocator sells sourcing access to the buy side. They are different audiences with opposite suppression rules, so they run as separate campaigns on separate lists.

PBExpo 2027 · March 10–11, 2027 · Miami Beach · 5 senders · ~500 connection requests per week across the pool
Reps: this is the copy that goes out under your name. Nothing on this page sends until you have read it. If a line does not sound like you, say so and it gets changed before launch. That was the commitment and it stands.
  • You are not running these campaigns. Marketing operates them end to end.
  • Every reply is triaged before it reaches you. Out-of-office, not interested and wrong-person never land on your desk.
  • A qualified conversation arrives as a HubSpot task with a four-hour response window. That is the only step that costs you time.
  • You can withdraw at any point and the campaign reroutes across the remaining profiles.
Four design decisions worth knowing before you read the copy
  • The connection request carries no note. LinkedIn caps personalised notes on free accounts at roughly three to five a month, so a note-bearing sequence stops dead after three sends. Bare requests also accept better, 55–68% against 28–45% for generic notes. The personalisation moves into the first message, where it has 300+ characters instead of 200.
  • If connected is step one on both. Reps are already connected to a meaningful share of these people, especially past PBExpo registrants. Without the branch they receive a redundant request and drop out of the campaign.
  • Suppression is inverted between the two. Existing PartsBase customers are excluded from PBLocator because they already have access, and included in PBExpo because supply-side customers are the warmest booth prospects in the file.
  • Capacity is shared, not added. Five senders at 20–25 requests a day is 500–625 a week across the pool, split across whatever is running. PBExpo runs first and PBLocator follows it.

Checklist

  • Rep name and signature set on each sending profile. No rep invented.
  • The {HOOK} column generated and spot-checked. Read fifty. A bad hook is worse than no hook.
  • Fallbacks filled on every variable. A failed variable mid-message is the most visible automation tell there is.
  • The 86% decision-maker claim confirmed or cut.
  • A target set for meetings booked per sender per week, so the first review has something to measure against.
  • Warm-up actions counted against the 40 per day cap, shared across View Profile, Follow and Like.
  • All five reps have read their copy and signed the consent agreement.
What to watch in week one
  • Acceptance rate tells you whether the targeting is right. Healthy is 40% and up on a bare request to a relevant audience.
  • Reply rate tells you whether the message is right. If acceptance is healthy and replies are flat, the problem is step 4, not the list.
  • Watch per sender, not just in aggregate. One profile performing far below the others usually means a cold account that needed more warming, not bad copy.
