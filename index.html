Strategy playbook
Synthesized from 19 vetted marketing skills + current B2B-event benchmarks. Every play serves one message.
🎯 The one message — "Walk in with a pipeline, not a floor map."
The app's AI matchmaking pre-books a schedule of best-fit meetings before arrival. Teams want qualified leads, not badges. The AI is the how; the pipeline is the what — lead every channel with the outcome. Free event, so sell time-ROI ("two days that replace a quarter of prospecting"), not price.
Top plays — highest leverage
1 · One message everywhere
Outcome-led hero + jobs-to-be-done framing on every email, ad, and the landing page. Meetings, not badges.
2 · Pre-target → reg-open one-two punch (Aug 18)
Warm the matchmaking idea Aug 1–17, convert the same audience on open day. Sequencing beats spend (Rule of 7).
3 · Zeigarnik + loss-aversion engine
"Your AI matches are waiting / others are booking your buyers" across email + retargeting — cheap, repeatable, warm-audience.
4 · Persona-split the one promise (A/B/C)
Pipeline (new buyers) · efficiency-vs-last-year (past attendees) · two-sided matching (Career Runway). One engine, three jobs.
5 · Commitment-then-upgrade
Build the free match-list first, then anchor $1,000 → $500 as "premium visibility to buyers you already matched with."
6 · Before/After hero creative
"Floor map → booked pipeline" — a crumpled map vs a phone of pre-booked, fit-scored meetings. The campaign's visual spine.
7 · Flagship guide → lead magnet → 8+ assets
"How to walk into PBExpo with a pipeline" ranks, gates leads, and repurposes into carousels/threads/emails.
8 · Fix SSR + Event/FAQ schema Eng dependency
Server-render title/H1/body or organic + AI-citation = zero. Gates the whole SEO channel — a concrete engineering ask for Rodrigo.

Sequencing — one important flag
⚠️ 6-month runway is longer than the 12–16-week best practice. Run a low-frequency awareness phase Aug–Dec (teasers, reveals, matchmaking education), then ramp hard urgency in the final 4–6 weeks (Jan–Mar) — or you'll fatigue the list. No price lever, so substitute meeting-slot scarcity + calendar-ROI as the FOMO engine. Matchmaking needs critical mass early → add a "reserve your matches" post-registration CTA to keep the long runway warm.

SMS · two flows

Sent to the PartsBase SMS opt-in list, dated against the live comms calendar. Flow A drives registration and suppresses anyone already registered; registering moves the contact out of A and into B

Why these dates. All three acquisition texts sit inside the Sep–Dec window and land on days with nothing else scheduled — the calendar loads Wednesday to Friday and leaves Mondays and Tuesdays almost entirely empty, so nobody gets an email and a text the same morning. They target the two thinnest months rather than spreading evenly: September carries two attendee sends all month and October has nothing until the 21st. November is skipped on purpose, being the healthiest month in the window.

Why the fonts are what they are

The template text slots use Bruno Ace Regular for the headline and Roboto Medium for the call-to-action line. That was a constraint, not a preference: Avenir Next from the brand guidelines is not available in the Figma environment, and Figma refuses to write text into a layer whose font it cannot load.

It turned out to be a useful constraint. Bruno Ace and Roboto are both Google Fonts, freely licensed, so the automated renderer uses the genuine font files and reproduces the template exactly. Had the slots used Avenir Next — a licensed font — the renderer would have to substitute a lookalike and the layouts would drift from the approved design.

One consequence worth knowing: the headline slot holds three lines at 34pt, which is about 47 characters. Copy is capped at 45 to leave headroom. Anything longer is flagged as overflowing and sent back for a shorter headline rather than rendered clipped. Longer headlines are a template decision, not a copywriting one.

What stops a bad ad
Before a human sees it
• Every number in the copy must trace to a claim the requester supplied — invented figures are rejected
• No superlatives ("best", "largest", "only") unless that exact claim was supplied
• Aviation terms stay upper-cased: AOG, MRO, OEM, 8130
• No fake urgency, no emoji, no ALL-CAPS emphasis
• Never promises anything about compliance, traceability or certification
• Landing URL must carry full UTM tagging or the request is blocked
• Copy that would render outside its slot is flagged, not shipped
Before anything spends money
• Approval in Teams is required — nothing proceeds without a recorded verdict
• Launching is a separate system with its own switch, off by default
• Campaigns are created as drafts, never live
• A single kill-switch file halts every write across the whole system
• Optimisation logic only ever proposes changes; applying them is a distinct, gated step
• Every change is recorded so it can be reversed
• Rejected creative is regenerated, and a repeatedly rejected ad escalates to a person instead of looping
Honest limits
Imagery is fixed, not generated. The AI writes words; it never invents visuals. Ads are built on pre-approved templates, which is what keeps brand fidelity intact and lets copy be measured against a constant creative shell.

The images sit one click from the approval card, not inside it. Teams fetches card images anonymously, and every storage option currently available refuses anonymous requests. The card links to a page showing all three ads instead. Fixing this needs a storage account; it does not block anything.

Templates are a snapshot. The renderer holds a copy of the design and its measurements. If a template changes, that copy has to be refreshed or ads keep using the old design.
