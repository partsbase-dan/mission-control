In one paragraph

We are adding LinkedIn as a second outbound channel alongside email, using a tool called HeyReach. One person in Marketing runs the campaigns centrally across five sales reps' LinkedIn profiles. The reps lend their professional identity, which is what makes the outreach credible to an aviation buyer, and receive qualified replies as handoffs. They do not build, schedule or manage anything. Total tool cost is $395 a month. It goes live in October and every touch is logged in HubSpot, so it is measurable from week one.

Why add a second channel now

Three reasons, in order of weight.

1. Email is being rebuilt, and one channel is a single point of failure

We are in the middle of repairing years of damage to our email deliverability. That work is going well, but it is deliberately slow: volume has to ramp gradually to rebuild reputation with the inbox providers. For the next quarter, email alone cannot carry outbound. A second channel that does not depend on our sending domain gives the quarter a floor.

These two programmes do not interact

LinkedIn does not touch our email sending domain, so nothing about this can affect the deliverability rebuild happening at the same time. The two share a contact database and nothing else. That is why this can run in October rather than waiting.

2. Our buyers are reachable there

Procurement, supply chain and maintenance leadership at MROs, distributors, airlines and defense contractors maintain active LinkedIn profiles in a way they do not maintain public email addresses. For the segments we care most about, it is often the only reliable route to a named decision maker.

3. It is cheap to test and cheap to stop

$395 a month, month to month, with no integration work required beyond what we are already building. If it does not produce meetings in the first quarter, we stop and we have lost very little.

Why LinkedIn outreach usually fails, and what we are doing differently

Plain English: how these tools normally work

Nearly every LinkedIn outreach tool assumes each sales rep logs in and runs their own campaigns: builds the list, writes the messages, schedules the sequence, monitors replies. It is a marketing job handed to someone paid to close deals.

That model fails predictably. Reps are measured on revenue, not campaign admin, so the work gets deprioritised in week two and the campaigns quietly stop. Anyone who has rolled out a sales engagement tool has watched this happen. It is not a discipline problem, it is a job design problem.

We have already seen this here

Rep-driven sequencing has been attempted at PartsBase before and did not sustain. Repeating the same structure with a different vendor would produce the same outcome.

The agency model

Plain English: what "run it like an agency" means

HeyReach lets one operator run campaigns across several LinkedIn accounts at once from a single console, with all replies landing in one shared inbox. So Marketing does the work an outside agency would do, and the reps supply only the thing an agency cannot: a real, credible professional identity in the aviation industry.

This is the specific reason HeyReach was chosen over the alternatives. It is a structural fit with how we want to operate, not a feature comparison.

What we are asking of the five reps

This is the part that needs leadership awareness, because it involves personal accounts.

The outreach goes out from reps' own LinkedIn profiles. That is what makes it work, and it is also a genuine ask, so it is being handled formally rather than informally. Before anything is connected, each rep signs a written agreement covering:

  • Consent and scope. Explicit agreement to send on their behalf, and exactly what kind of message goes out.
  • Copy approval. The right to review and reject anything going out in their name.
  • Privacy. Who can see what in their inbox. Outreach replies arrive alongside recruiter messages and personal conversations, and this is the clause reps care about most.
  • Access handling. How the connection is made and that it ends when they change roles or leave.
  • A clean exit. Any rep can withdraw at any time and the campaign reroutes across the remaining profiles. No one is locked in.
HR is involved before anything is signed

This is a personal-account access question, not only an IT one. The policy goes through HR review first so that reps are being asked properly, and so the company is covered.

How much outreach this actually is

Plain English: why there is a ceiling

LinkedIn enforces limits on how many people an account can approach, and exceeding them gets the account restricted or banned. This is not a setting we can turn up. Serious outreach scales by adding accounts, not by pushing any single account harder, which is exactly why a five-profile pool matters.

20-25New contacts per rep, per day
~500Across the pool, per week
~2,000Per month

Volume starts below these numbers and builds. Accounts are warmed before campaigns run at full rate, and the platform assigns each profile a dedicated connection and freezes any account approaching a risky threshold automatically. The intent is that no rep's personal LinkedIn account is ever put at risk, and that constraint is treated as non-negotiable rather than as a target to push against.

How we will know if it is working

Every LinkedIn action is logged against the contact record in HubSpot: connection sent, connection accepted, message sent, reply received. This channel reports into the same system as everything else rather than sitting in a separate dashboard, which means it is measurable from the first week instead of being a black box we assess in six months.

A target for meetings per sender per week is set before launch, so the first review has something to measure against rather than a narrative.
